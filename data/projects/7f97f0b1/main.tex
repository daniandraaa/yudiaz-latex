\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Predicting whether an idiopathic outflow-tract ventricular arrhythmia (OT-VA) originates from the left (LVOT) or right (RVOT) ventricular outflow tract guides the ablation approach. Hybrid CNN--Transformer models are attractive for this task, but the conventional design, which assigns one attention token to every time step of the CNN feature map, is computationally expensive.
\textbf{Methods:} We evaluated CNN-PatchTST, a hybrid in which CNN features are grouped into 128-ms patch tokens before self-attention, on the public Ningbo 12-lead ECG database (289 patients with ablation-confirmed origin). It was compared with a conventional CNN--Transformer encoder, a hierarchical CNN--Transformer, two ablations (CNN-only and PatchTST-only), a tuned bidirectional LSTM, a feature-based random forest and three clinical ECG criteria. Models were assessed by patient-level 5-fold cross-validation repeated with three seeds (230 patients) using a corrected repeated cross-validation $t$-test, and on a 59-patient historical hold-out set. Floating-point operations and inference latency were measured under identical conditions.
\textbf{Results:} CNN-PatchTST achieved a cross-validated macro-F1 of 0.899 (95\% CI 0.865--0.933) and AUC of 0.940 (0.916--0.965), comparable to the best conventional hybrid (encoder: 0.902 and 0.939), with the smallest between-fold variability. No difference from any deep comparator was statistically significant. A balanced audit, in which the ablations received the same hyper-parameter search, showed that the CNN stem added no measurable accuracy: CNN-only (0.903) and PatchTST-only (0.900) matched the hybrid, results were insensitive to patch length (64--256~ms) and the models made nearly identical patient-level predictions ($\kappa$ 0.89--0.92). CNN-PatchTST outperformed the random forest (+0.124 macro-F1, Holm-adjusted $p=0.043$) and all clinical criteria (AUC difference +0.12 to +0.21). Patch tokenization shortened the attention sequence from 312 to 20 tokens, reducing floating-point operations 2.9--3.4-fold, inference latency 2.5--3.0-fold on GPU (4.1 vs 10.2--12.2~ms per patient) and 2.1--2.4-fold on CPU (8.4 vs 17.8--20.6~ms), and training time 3.1-fold relative to the conventional hybrids; it was about 7-fold faster than the BiLSTM.
\textbf{Conclusion:} Patch tokenization retains the accuracy of conventional CNN--Transformer models for LVOT/RVOT localization while substantially reducing computational cost; however, simpler patch-based or convolutional models reach the same accuracy at even lower cost, indicating that architectural complexity is not the limiting factor for this task. External validation is required before clinical use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Idiopathic ventricular arrhythmias most often arise from the ventricular outflow tracts. Catheter ablation is effective, but the procedure is planned differently when the origin is in the left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT), including the aortic cusps, than when it is in the right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT). Several surface-ECG criteria have therefore been proposed, such as the precordial transition zone, the V2 transition ratio \cite{betensky2011v2tr} and the V2S/V3R index \cite{yoshida2014v2sv3r}. These rules are simple and interpretable, but they rely on manual measurements, cannot be applied in every patient, and lose accuracy when the precordial transition occurs in lead V3.

Machine-learning and deep-learning models have been developed to automate this decision \cite{zheng2022origins,zhao2022features,nakasone2024dualrhythm,shahsavari2024origin,kujime2024semantic,talebzadeh2025outflow,baek2026origin,miyamae2026paired}. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) capture local waveform morphology, whereas Transformers \cite{vaswani2017attention} model long-range dependencies through self-attention. Hybrid CNN--Transformer models combine both properties and have been used for several ECG tasks \cite{zou2023dwtcnntrn,kim2025stockwell,tang2024hierarchical}. In the conventional hybrid design, however, every time step of the CNN feature map becomes an attention token. For a 2.5-s, 2000-Hz window our CNN produces 312 tokens of 8~ms each, so each attention layer evaluates about $10^5$ token pairs, and individual tokens represent fragments of a waveform rather than physiologically meaningful units.

PatchTST \cite{nie2023patchtst} addressed a similar problem in time-series forecasting by grouping consecutive time steps into patches that serve as tokens. Patching preserves local semantic information within each token and reduces the cost of attention quadratically for the same input length. In ECG analysis, a patch of about 100--150~ms approximates the duration of a QRS complex, so a patch token can represent one morphological unit. Unlike the original channel-independent PatchTST, localization of arrhythmia origin depends on relations between leads, such as the R/S transition across V1--V6 on which the clinical criteria are based. We therefore form each patch from all 12 leads jointly, an approach also used by other 12-lead ECG Transformers \cite{cheng2023mswtransformer}.

Prior studies on the public Ningbo database \cite{zheng2020dataset} have reported high accuracy, but comparisons are complicated by beat-level or unclear data splits \cite{talebzadeh2025outflow}, exclusion of undecidable recordings \cite{kujime2024semantic}, or model selection involving the test cohort \cite{zheng2022origins}. To our knowledge, no study has compared CNN--Transformer tokenization strategies, component ablations, competitive non-CNN models and clinical criteria within a single patient-level protocol.

This study makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item We introduce CNN-PatchTST for LVOT/RVOT localization and compare it with a conventional CNN--Transformer encoder and a hierarchical CNN--Transformer under an identical patient-level protocol.
\item We show that patch tokenization shortens the attention sequence from 312 to 20 tokens, reducing attention entries 152--243-fold and floating-point operations 2.9--3.4-fold relative to the conventional hybrids, with 2.1--3.1-fold faster inference and training and comparable accuracy.
\item A balanced audit of the CNN contribution (equal hyper-parameter search for the ablations, patch-length and stride sensitivity, prediction agreement and paired hold-out analysis) tests whether the hybrid design is needed.
\item CNN-PatchTST is benchmarked against a tuned bidirectional LSTM, a feature-based random forest and three clinical ECG criteria, with an analysis of errors by anatomical site.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dataset}
The study workflow is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}. We used the public 12-lead ECG database of Ningbo First Hospital \cite{zheng2020dataset} (CC0 licence), recorded at 2000~Hz from patients who underwent successful catheter ablation of idiopathic ventricular arrhythmia. The site of successful ablation served as the reference label. LVOT comprised the aorto-mitral continuity, left coronary cusp, right coronary cusp and LCC--RCC commissure; RVOT comprised the anterior, left and right cusps, the anterior and posterior septum, the free wall and other RVOT sites. Patients with summit or unknown origin were excluded, leaving 289 patients (71 LVOT, 218 RVOT; Table~\ref{tab:cohort}). Recordings lasted a median of 11.8~s (range 2.9--59.3~s).

A patient-level 20\% hold-out set (59 patients) had been defined before this study and was used in earlier exploratory experiments; we therefore refer to it as a \emph{historical hold-out} and treat it as secondary evidence. All model development and selection used the remaining 230 development patients.

\subsection{Pre-processing}
Raw signals were high-pass filtered at 0.5~Hz (fourth-order zero-phase Butterworth) and denoised with a five-level coif5 wavelet transform using per-level SURE soft thresholding. Each recording was segmented into 2.5-s windows (5000 samples) with 50\% overlap, giving 2274 development windows (median 8 per patient, range 1--46) and 533 hold-out windows. Lead-wise standardization parameters were estimated on the training patients of each fold only.

\subsection{Models}
All deep models share the same classification head (layer normalization, a 256$\rightarrow$128 linear layer with ReLU, dropout 0.3 and a linear output layer) and the same patient-level aggregation: the predicted probability of a patient is the mean of the probabilities of all of its windows \cite{ilse2018mil}. Architectures are summarized in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}.

\paragraph{Shared CNN stem} Four 1-D convolutional blocks (32, 64, 128 and 256 channels; kernel sizes 7, 5, 3 and 3), each followed by batch normalization, ReLU and max-pooling with stride 2. One output feature covers 54 samples (27~ms), and a 5000-sample window yields 312 feature vectors at 8-ms intervals.

\paragraph{CNN-Transformer Encoder} The 312 CNN feature vectors are used directly as tokens with sinusoidal positional encoding and passed through two Transformer encoder layers (4 heads, feed-forward dimension 1024, dropout 0.2), followed by mean pooling.

\paragraph{CNN-Hierarchical Transformer} A local Transformer layer over the 312 tokens is followed by a stride-2 convolution (156 tokens) and a global Transformer layer, followed by mean pooling.

\paragraph{CNN-PatchTST (proposed)} Every 16 consecutive CNN feature vectors are concatenated into one patch (128~ms, all leads jointly) and linearly projected to 256 dimensions, giving 20 tokens per window; the final partial patch is zero-padded and down-weighted in pooling. Positional encoding, two Transformer layers and a length-weighted mean pooling follow.

\paragraph{Ablations} \emph{CNN-only} removes the Transformer and averages the CNN features over time. \emph{PatchTST-only} removes the CNN: the raw signal is cut into the same 20 patches of 256 samples $\times$ 12 leads, linearly projected and processed by the same Transformer and head.

\paragraph{Non-CNN comparators} A bidirectional LSTM \cite{hochreiter1997lstm,schuster1997bidirectional} (two layers, 128 hidden units per direction, dropout 0.2) processes each window after averaging every 8 samples (250~Hz, 625 steps), followed by mean pooling. A random forest \cite{breiman2001rf} (500 trees, balanced class weights, otherwise default settings) uses 132 handcrafted morphology features per patient (median and inter-quartile range across beats of QRS width, relative R and S amplitudes, R fraction and signed QRS area in each lead, plus QRS span, precordial transition, V2S/V3R and RR intervals), with median imputation estimated on the training folds.

\subsection{Training}
Models were trained with AdamW \cite{loshchilov2019adamw} (weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine learning-rate decay ($T_{\max}=100$, minimum $10^{-6}$), gradient clipping at 1.0, class weights inversely proportional to patient class frequency, up to 100 epochs and early stopping with patience 15 on validation patient-level macro-F1. During training, four windows were sampled per patient; batch sizes are expressed in windows (32 windows = 8 patients).

For every deep model trained end-to-end (the three hybrids and the BiLSTM), learning rate and batch size were selected with the same procedure on the binary task: ten candidates, namely a default configuration (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 32, no augmentation) and the grid $\{10^{-4}, 5\times10^{-4}, 10^{-3}\} \times \{16, 32, 64\}$ with augmentation, were evaluated by development cross-validation, and the candidate with the highest mean macro-F1 was retained. The selected configurations (learning rate/batch size, all with augmentation) were $10^{-4}$/32 for CNN-PatchTST, $5\times10^{-4}$/64 for the encoder, $10^{-3}$/64 for the hierarchical model and $10^{-3}$/16 for the BiLSTM. The two ablations received the same search (selected: $5\times10^{-4}$/16 for CNN-only and $10^{-4}$/64 for PatchTST-only); their results with the CNN-PatchTST setting are also reported. The random forest used default settings. During training, one of the four windows of each patient was replaced by a synthetic window of the same side generated by a class-conditional GAN \cite{goodfellow2014gan} trained only on the training patients of that fold and accepted after a pre-specified quality screen; the isolated effect of this augmentation was small and not statistically significant in preliminary experiments.

Final models were retrained on all 230 development patients for the median best epoch observed in cross-validation, with five seeds (42--46), and their probabilities were averaged (ensemble). The decision threshold was fixed at 0.5.

\subsection{Evaluation and statistics}
The primary evaluation was patient-level stratified-group 5-fold cross-validation repeated with three seeds (15 fits per model); every model used the same folds, seeds and validation patients, allowing paired comparisons. Metrics were macro-F1, AUC, balanced accuracy and LVOT sensitivity. Because cross-validation fits share training data, they are not independent; differences were therefore tested with the corrected repeated cross-validation $t$-test \cite{nadeau2003inference,bouckaert2004evaluating}, with variance inflated by $(1/J + n_{\text{test}}/n_{\text{train}})$, $J=15$, and $p$-values were adjusted with the Holm procedure across the six comparators for each metric. Non-inferiority of CNN-PatchTST was declared when the lower limit of the 90\% confidence interval of the difference exceeded $-0.03$ macro-F1 or $-0.04$ AUC, equivalent to a one-sided test at $\alpha=0.05$ \cite{schuirmann1987tost}; these margins were chosen after the mean cross-validation results were known and should be interpreted accordingly. On the hold-out set, 95\% confidence intervals were obtained by stratified patient bootstrap (5000 resamples) and paired predictions were compared with the exact McNemar test.

Clinical criteria were computed automatically on beats with QRS width $\geq$120~ms (and, for the V2 transition ratio, sinus beats with QRS width $<$120~ms): precordial transition zone (LVOT if R$\geq$S by V2, RVOT if at V4 or later, undecided at V3), V2S/V3R $\leq 1.5$ \cite{yoshida2014v2sv3r} and V2 transition ratio $\geq 0.6$ \cite{betensky2011v2tr}, using the published thresholds without tuning.

\subsection{Audit of the CNN contribution}
To test whether the hybrid design is needed, we performed four additional analyses. (i) Balanced tuning: CNN-only and PatchTST-only received the same ten-candidate search as the other deep models, followed by final models and hold-out evaluation. (ii) Patch length and stride: for CNN-PatchTST and PatchTST-only, patch lengths of 64, 128 and 256~ms were combined with a stride equal to the patch length (no overlap) or half of it (50\% overlap), using each model's selected training configuration and cross-validation only; the 128-ms non-overlapping setting was retrained with the same parameterized implementation, whose FLOPs were identical to the original model. (iii) Consistency and representation: between-fold variability, worst-fold macro-F1, the number of paired fits favouring CNN-PatchTST, and the agreement of pooled out-of-fold predictions (Cohen's $\kappa$, correlation of predicted probabilities, patients classified correctly by only one model, errors by site of origin). (iv) Hold-out re-evaluation: paired stratified bootstrap (5000 resamples) of the ensemble differences in macro-F1 and AUC with 90\% and 95\% intervals and the exact McNemar test; and a matched comparison with the clinical criteria restricted to the patients each criterion could classify, in which the model threshold was set to give the same LVOT specificity as the criterion.

\subsection{Computational efficiency}
All models were benchmarked on one laptop (Apple M4, PyTorch 2.12) in a single session on the same 30 development patients (mean 11.1 windows per patient); deep models were instantiated with random weights, because latency and FLOPs do not depend on weight values. We report parameters, floating-point operations (FLOPs) per window, attention-matrix entries per window, median latency per patient over 90 measurements after warm-up (all windows of a patient in one pass) on CPU and GPU (Metal), GPU throughput at batch size 64, and training time per cross-validation fit. Latency excludes raw-signal pre-processing, except for the random forest, whose timing includes feature extraction.

\subsection{Pre-registration and reproducibility}
Each experiment was specified in a time-stamped internal protocol, together with SHA-256 hashes of the analysis code, before the corresponding training or test evaluation; the code refused to run if modified afterwards. The hold-out set was never used for model or hyper-parameter selection. Reporting follows TRIPOD+AI \cite{collins2024tripodai}.

\subsection{Overall performance}
Table~\ref{tab:main} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cv} summarize performance. In cross-validation, the tuned deep models reached mean macro-F1 values between 0.893 and 0.903: CNN-only 0.903, the encoder 0.902, PatchTST-only 0.900, CNN-PatchTST 0.899, the hierarchical model 0.894 and the BiLSTM 0.893. CNN-PatchTST had the highest mean AUC (0.940) and, together with CNN-only, the smallest between-fold standard deviation of macro-F1 (0.028, compared with 0.029--0.049 for the other deep models and 0.081 for the random forest). On the historical hold-out set, all deep models had overlapping confidence intervals (macro-F1 0.81--0.87, AUC 0.94--0.98), whereas the random forest was clearly lower (macro-F1 0.721, AUC 0.741).

\subsection{Statistical comparison}
No difference between CNN-PatchTST and the other deep models was statistically significant (corrected $p=0.47$--$0.98$; Table~\ref{tab:stats}, Fig.~\ref{fig:forest}). In AUC, CNN-PatchTST was non-inferior to all deep comparators at the 0.04 margin. \subsection{Computational efficiency}
CNN-PatchTST required 0.37 GFLOPs per window, compared with 1.24 for the encoder and 1.06 for the hierarchical model, and computed 800 attention entries per window instead of 194,688 and 121,680 (Table~\ref{tab:eff}, Fig.~\ref{fig:eff}). Its median latency was 4.1~ms per patient on GPU and 8.4~ms on CPU, which was 2.5--3.0 times faster on GPU and 2.1--2.4 times faster on CPU than the conventional hybrids, and 7.1--7.6 times faster than the BiLSTM. GPU throughput was 2875 windows per second (820 for the encoder and 1004 for the hierarchical model), and one cross-validation fit took 0.64~min (1.98--1.99~min for the conventional hybrids). A complete recording, on average 27.7~s of ECG, was therefore processed roughly three orders of magnitude faster than real time on a CPU. CNN-PatchTST has more parameters than the other hybrids (2.80 vs 1.75--1.95 million) because of its patch projection, but parameter count did not determine cost: sequence length and FLOPs did (Fig.~\ref{fig:flops}). The two ablations, which reached the same accuracy, were cheaper still: CNN-only required 0.26 GFLOPs and 5.5~ms per patient on CPU, and PatchTST-only 0.09 GFLOPs and 1.8~ms.

\subsection{Comparison with clinical criteria}
The clinical criteria could be applied in 54--97\% of patients and achieved AUCs of 0.75--0.84 in the development cohort (Table~\ref{tab:clinical}). Using pooled out-of-fold predictions, CNN-PatchTST reached an AUC of 0.959, exceeding the transition zone by 0.21 (95\% CI 0.13--0.29), V2S/V3R by 0.12 (0.06--0.19) and the V2 transition ratio by 0.14 (0.07--0.22). On the hold-out set the differences were in the same direction (+0.05 to +0.11) but not statistically significant (Fig.~\ref{fig:roc}). \section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Principal findings}
In a patient-level benchmark in which every deep model received the same hyper-parameter search, CNN-PatchTST reached accuracy comparable to conventional CNN--Transformer hybrids, its ablations and a tuned BiLSTM, at a fraction of the computational cost of the conventional hybrids. The audit showed, however, that the CNN stem did not add measurable accuracy: all tuned deep models converged to a macro-F1 of about 0.90, made nearly identical predictions and failed on the same patients. The main finding is therefore that, for LVOT/RVOT discrimination on this database, architectural complexity is not the limiting factor; patch tokenization reduces the cost of attention, and simpler models are equally accurate.

\subsection{Why patch tokens}
Patch tokenization reduces the attention sequence from 312 to 20 tokens. Since self-attention scales quadratically with sequence length, this explains the 243-fold reduction in attention entries and the 3.4-fold reduction in FLOPs relative to the encoder (152-fold and 2.9-fold relative to the hierarchical model), consistent with the original PatchTST analysis \cite{nie2023patchtst}. A 128-ms patch approximates the duration of a QRS complex, which motivated the design, but the sensitivity analysis showed that patches of 64--256~ms performed equally well, so this correspondence should be regarded as a design rationale rather than a demonstrated optimum. Forming patches from all 12 leads jointly keeps the inter-lead relations on which LVOT/RVOT discrimination relies.

\subsection{What does the CNN stem contribute?}
With equal tuning, local convolutional features alone (CNN-only, 0.903) and patch-level context from raw samples alone (PatchTST-only, 0.900) matched their combination (0.899). The hybrid was not more stable than CNN-only (identical standard deviation), its predictions agreed with those of the ablations for all but 3--5 patients, and it was not better on the difficult aortic-cusp origins. The only residual advantages were a slightly higher pooled AUC and a higher worst-fold macro-F1 than CNN-only, both small. The apparent benefit of the hybrid in our first ablation came from tuning the ablations with the hybrid's configuration. We conclude that, for this task and data size, the CNN stem is not required; PatchTST-only offers the lowest cost (1.8~ms per patient on CPU) and CNN-only the smallest model (0.17 million parameters). The hybrid was also more sensitive to the learning rate than the simpler models, which is a practical disadvantage.

\subsection{Comparison with other approaches}
The tuned BiLSTM reached a similar macro-F1 but was about 7 times slower, because it processes 625 time steps sequentially. The random forest on handcrafted morphology features performed worst among the learned models, particularly for LVOT (sensitivity 0.62), suggesting that summary features lose information that end-to-end models exploit. All learned deep models exceeded the clinical criteria, which could not be applied in every patient.

Table~\ref{tab:sota} places the results in the context of previous work. Direct comparison is not possible because evaluation units, splits and exclusions differ: Talebzadeh et al.\ \cite{talebzadeh2025outflow} reported 93.4\% accuracy with an 80/20 split after oversampling without specifying whether the split was patient-level; Kujime et al.\ \cite{kujime2024semantic} reported an AUC of 0.848 on the Ningbo database after excluding 28.4\% of recordings as undecidable; and Zheng et al.\ \cite{zheng2022origins} used a larger cohort with model selection involving the testing cohort. Our study classifies every patient, uses patient-level folds and keeps the hold-out set out of all selection steps.

\subsection{Clinical interpretation}
Errors concentrated in the right and left coronary cusps. The right coronary cusp lies immediately posterior to the RVOT, and arrhythmias from this region can produce RVOT-like morphology; similar difficulties are recognized for manual criteria. Because CNN-PatchTST runs in under 10~ms per patient on a CPU and occupies 11~MB, it could be embedded in electrophysiology workstations to provide immediate pre-procedural guidance without dedicated hardware.

\subsection{Why efficiency matters when all models are fast}
All deep models processed a patient in well under a second, so the latency gain alone is not clinically decisive for a single 12-lead ECG. The practical value of the reduction lies elsewhere. First, training cost fell about 3-fold, which matters for repeated cross-validation, hyper-parameter search and future multicentre retraining. Second, recordings are processed window by window, so the per-window saving accumulates linearly over long recordings (e.g., screening hours of Holter data for ectopic beats), and attention cost within a window grows quadratically with the number of tokens: patching divides the token count by 16 and the attention term by 256, so the advantage widens if longer analysis windows are needed to capture more beats. Third, low FLOPs and CPU-only inference enable deployment on bedside devices, mapping systems and wearable or low-power hardware, and allow large ECG archives to be screened retrospectively at negligible cost.

\subsection{Limitations}
This study has limitations. First, all data came from a single centre and no external validation was available; the results must be confirmed in independent cohorts. Second, the hold-out set had been observed in earlier experiments, so cross-validation is the primary evidence. Third, the non-inferiority margins were chosen after the cross-validation means were known, and non-inferiority to the encoder could not be shown at the 0.03 margin; full confidence intervals are reported so that readers can apply other margins. Fourth, hyper-parameters were selected in the same development cross-validation that is reported, which can make cross-validated estimates slightly optimistic; this applies equally to all tuned models. Fifth, the hold-out set is too small (14 LVOT patients) for paired non-inferiority testing between deep models; differences of one or two patients dominate. Sixth, our patch transformer mixes leads within each patch and differs from the channel-independent PatchTST. Seventh, the clinical criteria were measured automatically and not by electrophysiologists. Finally, absolute latencies depend on hardware; the relative differences and FLOPs are more transferable.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Grouping CNN features into 128-ms, lead-joint patch tokens preserved the accuracy of CNN--Transformer models for distinguishing LVOT from RVOT origin while reducing FLOPs 2.9--3.4-fold, inference latency 2.1--3.0-fold and training time about 3-fold. CNN-PatchTST did not differ significantly from any deep comparator and outperformed a feature-based random forest and standard clinical ECG criteria. A balanced audit showed that its CNN stem added no measurable accuracy and that simpler patch-based or convolutional models were equally accurate at lower cost. Lightweight models are therefore sufficient for real-time LVOT/RVOT decision support on standard hardware, pending external validation.
