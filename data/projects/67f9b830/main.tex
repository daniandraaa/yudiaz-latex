\begin{abstract}
An Internet of Things (IoT) service that localizes ventricular arrhythmia origins from 12-lead electrocardiograms (ECGs) must return the decision validated offline within a predictable processing time. This study develops a compact one-dimensional convolutional neural network (1D CNN) ensemble that classifies left versus right ventricular outflow-tract (LVOT/RVOT) origin at record level, and deploys it behind an HTTP/JSON gateway prototype with a browser dashboard. A Random Forest (RF) with 132 morphology and RR-interval features and three established ECG criteria serve as comparators. In patient-level cross-validation on 230 patients, the 1D CNN reached a macro-F1 of 0.903 against 0.775 for RF with the epoch selected on each validation fold, and 0.800 with a fixed epoch count. On a 59-patient historical hold-out not used for any model selection, the CNN ensemble outperformed RF in AUC (0.941 versus 0.741), LVOT sensitivity (0.857 versus 0.500), and macro-F1 (0.828 versus 0.721). Gateway replay preserved all 59 offline decisions. After a CNN latency tail was traced to a batch-size-dependent CPU slowdown and removed, the CNN pipeline took 57.8~ms (median) and 109.6~ms (95th percentile) per record with four threads, faster than RF, with a 3.3~MiB model footprint. Physical-device acquisition and external validation remain future work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Idiopathic premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) and ventricular tachycardia (VT) most often arise from the left or right ventricular outflow tract (LVOT or RVOT). Predicting the side before catheter ablation helps plan vascular access and procedure time, but manual criteria such as the V2 transition ratio~\cite{betensky2011} and V2S/V3R index~\cite{yoshida2014} depend on careful reading of precordial morphology. The public Ningbo database provides 12-lead PVC/VT recordings labelled with successful ablation sites~\cite{zheng2020}, enabling data-driven localization.

IoT ECG systems acquire signals on connected devices and analyze them on gateways or remote servers~\cite{satija2017,azariadi2016}. For such an IoT service, an accurate model is necessary but not sufficient. The service must (i) apply exactly the preprocessing used during model development to records that arrive in chunks over a network, (ii) return the same decision as the validated offline model, and (iii) do so within a bounded and predictable processing time. Most ECG learning studies report accuracy offline~\cite{hannun2019,ribeiro2020,zheng2021} and leave these deployment properties unmeasured.

This paper addresses localization and deployment together. Its contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A compact CNN pipeline for record-level LVOT/RVOT localization that, under matched patient partitions, exceeds a morphology-based RF and three established ECG criteria in macro-F1 and AUC. On a hold-out not used for selection, its ensemble also has significantly higher AUC and higher LVOT sensitivity than the RF ensemble.
\item An IoT gateway prototype (session declaration, sequenced chunks, completeness checks, server-side canonical preprocessing) with a verification procedure showing that gateway outputs reproduce offline model probabilities record by record.
\item A repeated latency benchmark that separates model inference from pipeline and gateway time. It identifies a batch-size-dependent CPU convolution slowdown as the cause of CNN tail latency, and shows that fixed 8-window inference batches remove it without changing any prediction.
\end{itemize}
The system performs post-acquisition localization of complete records. Experiments use real public-dataset recordings injected at the gateway; live physical-device acquisition is outside the evaluated scope.

\section{Related Work}
Conventional criteria express relationships among precordial morphologies: the transition zone, the V2 transition ratio~\cite{betensky2011}, and the V2S/V3R index~\cite{yoshida2014}. Zheng \emph{et al.} released the 334-patient database used here~\cite{zheng2020} and classified left/right origin with engineered features and machine learning~\cite{zheng2021}. Their representation differs from our 132-feature RF, which is therefore a local comparator with stated settings rather than a reproduction of their classifier.

Deep networks learn ECG representations directly for rhythm classification~\cite{hannun2019} and 12-lead diagnosis~\cite{ribeiro2020}, and have recently been applied to origin localization. Nakasone \emph{et al.} classified ventricular arrhythmia origins in 80 ablation patients with a visualized CNN~\cite{nakasone2022}. A multicenter model combining ResNet50 with a Transformer reached external accuracy 0.84 and F1 0.77 when PVC and sinus-rhythm beats were combined, and 0.74 and 0.68 with PVC morphology alone~\cite{nakasone2024}. Miyamae \emph{et al.} reported hold-out accuracy 92.1\% and F1 0.91 for PVCs with a V3 or V4 transition using paired PVC and intrinsic QRS inputs~\cite{miyamae2026}. Cohorts, inputs, and class definitions differ from ours, so these values give context rather than a ranking. On the IoT side, prior systems addressed signal-quality-aware ECG telemetry~\cite{satija2017} and arrhythmia detection on wearable devices~\cite{azariadi2016}, and Ardian and Mandala~\cite{ardian2025} coupled a Transformer-based beat classifier with a web application. The present work differs in output (record-level anatomical origin rather than beat class) and in evaluation: we verify decision preservation through the gateway and characterise latency tails with repeated measurements, rather than reporting offline accuracy alone.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Overview}
Fig.~\ref{fig:system} shows the three parts of the system: acquisition, a gateway and inference server, and a browser client. A complete 12-lead record enters the HTTP gateway, receives canonical preprocessing on the server, and is classified by the CNN ensemble; the RF comparator is timed separately, and the browser shows both outputs with stage timing. A 12-lead ECG recorder is the intended source; in the experiments, real public-dataset recordings are replayed into the gateway in its place.


\subsection{Dataset, Cohort Definition, and Class Imbalance}
The Ningbo resource contains 334 subjects recorded at 2000~Hz~\cite{zheng2020}. This study uses a cohort frozen in earlier work on the same models: five Summit records and 40 records without a \texttt{Sublocation} entry were excluded. All 334 subjects have a coarse left/right label, so the 40 exclusions are a cohort-definition choice, not missing outcomes. The frozen cohort was retained so that the fold assignment, historical hold-out, and deployed model artifacts remain identical across all analyses reported here; it also allows per-sublocation error analysis. Because the excluded records still have binary labels, their omission may make the cohort less representative; we discuss this in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

The 289 included patients comprise 71 LVOT and 218 RVOT cases (286 PVC and 3 VT records). Table~\ref{tab:cohort} gives the 230-patient development and 59-patient historical hold-out partitions; all splits are by patient. The 1:3 imbalance is handled in four ways: stratified folds, a class-weighted patient loss for the CNN, balanced class weights for RF, and reporting macro-F1, balanced accuracy, and LVOT sensitivity rather than accuracy alone. The three VT records are too few for separate analysis, so results describe a predominantly PVC population.

\subsection{ECG Preprocessing}
Leads are ordered I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1--V6 at native amplitude and sampling rate. The complete record is high-pass filtered (fourth-order Butterworth, 0.5~Hz, forward-backward) and denoised with a level-five coif5 wavelet transform using SURE soft thresholds~\cite{donoho1995}. The denoised signal is cut into complete 5000-point windows (2.5~s) with a 2500-point step, and each lead is standardized with statistics from training patients only. Forward-backward filtering and record-level wavelet thresholds are noncausal: they need the whole record. The deployed service therefore performs \emph{post-acquisition} localization. It returns one decision after the record is complete, not continuous beat-by-beat output. This suits the intended use, a pre-procedural origin estimate from a resting 12-lead recording, but would need causal filters for continuous monitoring.

\subsection{Compact CNN and Training}
Each CNN has four convolutional blocks with 32, 64, 128, and 256 channels and kernel lengths 7, 5, 3, and 3. Each block applies same-padded convolution, batch normalization, ReLU, and max pooling by two. Global temporal averaging feeds layer normalization, a 256-to-128 linear layer, ReLU, dropout 0.3, and a two-output layer. Each model has 170,914 trainable parameters. With window probabilities $p_{s,w}(c)$ from seed $s$ and window $w$, and $W_i$ complete windows in patient $i$, the single-model and ensemble probabilities are
\begin{equation}
\bar p_{i,s}(c)=\frac{1}{W_i}\sum_{w=1}^{W_i}p_{s,w}(c),\qquad
\hat p_i(c)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{s}\bar p_{i,s}(c).
\label{eq:pool}
\end{equation}
RVOT is assigned when $\hat p_i(\mathrm{RVOT})>0.5$.

Training samples bags of up to four windows per patient and applies a class-weighted negative log-likelihood after mean pooling. The bag size is a memory and diversity budget: random four-window draws expose the model to different parts of each recording across epochs. Because pooling is a mean of window probabilities, the training bag and the inference record estimate the same patient-level quantity. Inference averages all complete windows, which uses all available signal and reduces the variance of that estimate.

AdamW (weight decay $10^{-4}$), cosine decay to $10^{-6}$, and gradient clipping at 1.0 were used throughout. In CV, early stopping (patience 15) and checkpoint selection used macro-F1 on the outer validation fold of each split, which is also the fold that is scored; Section~\ref{sec:eval} describes how this is accounted for. Ten training configurations were evaluated: G00 without augmentation (learning rate $10^{-3}$, budget 32), specified in advance as the reference, and nine configurations G01--G09 crossing learning rates $\{10^{-4},5\times10^{-4},10^{-3}\}$ with window budgets $\{16,32,64\}$. G04 ($5\times10^{-4}$, 16) had the highest mean CV macro-F1 and was deployed. Five final CNNs (seeds 42--46) were fitted on all 230 development patients for a fixed 16 epochs, the median selected epoch of the G04 CV fits, without early stopping and without access to the hold-out.

\emph{Synthetic augmentation.} G01--G09 replace one of the four windows in a training bag with a synthetic window of the same class. Windows come from a class-conditional 1D convolutional generative adversarial network~\cite{mirza2014} (latent size 64, label embedding 16) trained for 100 epochs against a spectral-normalized projection discriminator with hinge loss. In CV, a separate generator was fitted for each fold using only that fold's training patients; the generator for the final models used only the 230 development patients. Validation folds, the hold-out, and gateway inference use real ECG only.

\subsection{Comparators}
\emph{Random Forest.} The RF~\cite{breiman2001} uses 132 features from the raw recording: per-lead QRS width, relative R and S amplitudes, R fraction, and signed QRS area; global QRS span, precordial transition, V2S/V3R; and three RR measures, each summarized by the median and interquartile range over detected beats. Missing values are median-imputed from training patients. Each forest has 500 trees, balanced class weights, and default scikit-learn settings otherwise; the RF was not tuned and is trained on real recordings only, with no synthetic data. It uses the same folds and seeds as the CNN, and deployment averages five forests.

\emph{Established ECG criteria.} Three published rules were computed without training on the same patients: transition zone (LVOT if $\le$V2, RVOT if $\ge$V4, indeterminate at V3), V2S/V3R index ($\le1.5$ indicates LVOT)~\cite{yoshida2014}, and V2 transition ratio ($\ge0.6$ indicates LVOT)~\cite{betensky2011}. Records where a criterion cannot be measured are assigned RVOT, the majority class.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval}
Five fixed stratified folds on the 230 development patients were evaluated with seeds 42--44, giving 15 paired fits per model. These fits share patients and folds and are not independent. We therefore report uncertainty at two levels. Fit-level paired differences use the corrected resampled $t$ statistic of Nadeau and Bengio~\cite{nadeau2003}. For patient-level uncertainty, the three seed predictions of each out-of-fold patient are averaged to give one probability per patient (230 in total). A class-stratified bootstrap over patients (5000 resamples) then gives intervals for the CNN-RF difference. Two selections make the CNN CV estimates optimistic: the epoch of each fit and the configuration G04 were both chosen on the scored folds. The RF involves neither. For the epoch, we add a fixed-epoch analysis from the stored per-epoch validation logs, without retraining: for each fold, the epoch count is the median selected epoch of the fits in the other four folds, the rule used for the final models, and the fold score is read at that epoch. Unless marked as fixed-epoch, CNN CV values use the selected epoch. All ten configurations are reported for transparency, and the hold-out, which played no role in any selection, provides the unbiased comparison. Hold-out ensemble differences use the same patient bootstrap and an exact McNemar test~\cite{mcnemar1947}. The hold-out had been inspected in earlier exploratory work and is secondary evidence, not an untouched test set.

\subsection{Web and IoT Deployment}
The service uses a Python HTTP server, PyTorch CNN inference, a scikit-learn RF comparator, and a browser dashboard served by the same host (Fig.~\ref{fig:browser}). Outside the loopback interface the server refuses to start without a bearer token, which every request must then carry. A client opens a session that declares sampling rate, lead order, amplitude unit, and expected length. It then sends sequenced JSON chunks (at most 8000 time points each) with sample offsets. Identical retransmissions are accepted once; gaps, conflicts, and inconsistent offsets are rejected. Finalization checks completeness before preprocessing and inference. CNN checkpoints and the scaler are verified against SHA-256 hashes. The CNN processes windows in fixed batches of 8 (Section~\ref{sec:latency}).

\emph{Consistency test.} With client and server on the same host (loopback interface), all 59 hold-out records were sent through the gateway in 1000-point chunks (0.5~s of signal each) without delay. Five records were also replayed at acquisition pace (2000 points/s). Gateway probabilities were compared with offline probabilities.

\emph{Latency benchmark.} Each of the 59 hold-out records was processed ten times after one warm-up, alternating CNN-first and RF-first order. Runs used 1 and 4 PyTorch threads, giving 1180 timed trials per thread setting. CNN pipeline time is preprocessing plus five-model inference; RF pipeline time is feature extraction plus five-forest inference. File I/O, network, browser, and model loading are excluded and reported separately from gateway timing. All runs used one Apple Silicon (arm64) workstation, macOS 26.2, PyTorch 2.12.0, CPU inference, and one job per forest.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Development Cross-Validation}
Table~\ref{tab:cv} compares the 1D CNN, RF, and the three ECG criteria on the 230 development patients. The 1D CNN reaches macro-F1 0.903 compared with 0.775 for RF, a paired difference of 0.128 (corrected 95\% CI 0.025 to 0.231, $p=0.019$). The CNN has higher macro-F1 in 13 of 15 fits and ties in two. The largest gain is in the minority class: LVOT sensitivity is 0.859 for CNN and 0.620 for RF. RVOT sensitivity is 0.950 and 0.915. With a fixed epoch count, the CNN reaches macro-F1 0.800 and AUC 0.888, still above RF (0.775 and 0.851) and above every ECG criterion, although the fit-level difference is no longer significant (macro-F1 difference 0.025, 95\% CI $-0.131$ to 0.180). The best ECG criterion (transition zone) reaches macro-F1 0.732; V2S/V3R gives 0.711 and the V2 transition ratio 0.500.

\emph{Patient-level uncertainty.} With one out-of-fold probability per patient (ensemble rows of Table~\ref{tab:cv}), the CNN reaches macro-F1 0.899 and AUC 0.946, against 0.779 and 0.842 for RF. The CNN value is essentially the fit mean (0.903); averaging seeds mainly raises AUC. This seed sensitivity motivates the five-model CNN ensemble (Section~\ref{sec:holdout}).

\subsection{Historical Hold-Out}
\label{sec:holdout}
Table~\ref{tab:holdout} and Fig.~\ref{fig:cm} summarize the deployed ensembles on 59 patients. Because neither the epoch count nor any other setting was chosen on these patients, this is the least biased CNN-RF comparison in the study. The CNN correctly classifies 12 of 14 LVOT and 39 of 45 RVOT cases; RF classifies 7 LVOT and 41 RVOT cases correctly. The CNN gains in LVOT sensitivity (0.857 versus 0.500) at the cost of two RVOT cases. The AUC difference is 0.200 (95\% CI 0.067 to 0.349). The macro-F1 difference is 0.108 (95\% CI $-0.026$ to 0.257; McNemar $p=0.55$). With 14 LVOT patients and a previously inspected hold-out, this is supporting rather than confirmatory evidence. Individual CNNs reach a mean macro-F1 of 0.793 (SD 0.038) and AUC of 0.916; the five-model ensemble raises these to 0.828 and 0.941 and removes the seed-to-seed spread from the deployed decision. The cost is five times the parameters (854,570) and five forward passes, which take a median of 33.5~ms per record in total (Section~\ref{sec:latency}). The single-model hold-out macro-F1 (0.793) agrees with the fixed-epoch CV estimate (0.800), as expected for models trained with a fixed epoch count, and the ensemble (0.828) lies between the fixed-epoch and selected-epoch CV values.

\subsection{Gateway Consistency and Application}
All 59 no-delay replays completed (record durations 5.1--25.0~s, median 12.9~s). Gateway and offline class predictions agree for 59/59 records for both models. The maximum absolute probability difference is $2.5\times10^{-7}$ for the CNN and zero for RF. All five acquisition-paced replays also preserve both predictions. This 100\% agreement measures implementation consistency: the gateway reproduces the validated model. Diagnostic accuracy remains that of Table~\ref{tab:holdout}. During paced replay, the median chunk round trip was 10.2~ms and the largest within-record chunk P95 was 51.6~ms, well below the 500~ms of signal each chunk represents. The transfer path therefore keeps pace with acquisition over loopback; a physical network adds latency that was not measured. Eleven automated tests exercise the real server and frozen models. They cover valid transfer, duplicate chunks, gap and conflict rejection, incomplete records, incompatible rate/lead/unit declarations, prediction parity, and result export; all pass.

Fig.~\ref{fig:browser} shows the application as opened in a web browser. The overview page provides record selection or CSV upload, transfer speed, and the localization result. The comparison page presents the CNN and RF development CV metrics and out-of-fold confusion matrices. \subsection{Processing Latency}
\label{sec:latency}
The first single-pass gateway measurement showed a lower CNN median but a long tail (P95 2370.6~ms against 254.4~ms for RF). Repeated measurements located the cause. The service ran CNN inference in batches of 16 windows, and on this PyTorch CPU build one-dimensional convolution becomes about 20 times slower once a batch holds 16 or more windows (one model: 15~ms for 15 windows, 371~ms for 16). Only records of at least 16 windows (about 21~s; 4 of 59 records) filled a full batch, which produced the tail. Processing windows in fixed batches of 8 removes the slowdown. Because the CNN evaluates each window independently, this does not change the output: class predictions agree in 1180/1180 trials, and the maximum probability difference is $2.5\times10^{-7}$.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The CNN outperforms RF in macro-F1, AUC, and balanced accuracy at both evaluation levels. The advantage is clearest in discrimination: the CNN has the higher AUC in CV with selected (0.935 versus 0.851) and fixed epochs (0.888), and on the hold-out the AUC difference of 0.200 excludes zero. On the hold-out the CNN also recovers 12 of 14 LVOT patients against 7 for RF, a clinically relevant gain for the minority class. The macro-F1 margin depends on how the CV epoch is chosen: it is 0.128 with the selected epoch and 0.025 with a fixed epoch, and on the hold-out it is 0.108 without excluding zero. The CNN is also faster and three times smaller than RF in deployment (Section~\ref{sec:latency}), so for this gateway it provides better discrimination at lower computational cost. A plausible explanation is that convolutional filters capture QRS morphology across all 12 leads that the fixed feature set summarizes only partially, although this study does not test that mechanism. The established ECG criteria trade LVOT sensitivity against specificity and do not reach the CNN's macro-F1 or AUC.

Several limitations remain. First, the RF used default hyperparameters and no gradient-boosting baseline was evaluated. Tuned feature-based models could narrow the gap, so conclusions are limited to the configurations evaluated here. Second, neither epoch nor configuration selection was nested in CV. The fixed-epoch analysis removes the epoch selection, and the hold-out is free of both, but nested cross-validation would give a less biased CV estimate. Third, the data come from one center, the hold-out had been inspected before, and only 14 hold-out patients are LVOT. Excluding 40 labelled records and having only three VT cases further limit how well the results generalize. An independent cohort is needed for confirmation. Fourth, the IoT evaluation verifies the gateway, its decision preservation, and its timing over loopback with replayed public recordings. It does not cover physical-recorder acquisition, amplitude calibration across devices, wireless loss, concurrent clients, energy use, or transport encryption (TLS) and access control beyond a bearer token. Finally, noncausal preprocessing restricts the service to post-acquisition localization, and probabilities are not calibrated risk estimates.

\section{Conclusion}
A compact 1D CNN ensemble was developed for record-level LVOT/RVOT localization and deployed behind a verified web-based IoT gateway. In patient-level CV the CNN reached macro-F1 0.903 with the epoch selected on each validation fold and 0.800 with a fixed epoch count, against 0.775 for a morphology-based Random Forest and at most 0.732 for established ECG criteria. On a historical hold-out not used for selection, the CNN ensemble had higher AUC (0.941 versus 0.741), LVOT sensitivity (0.857 versus 0.500), and macro-F1 (0.828 versus 0.721). The gateway reproduced all 59 offline decisions. With fixed 8-window inference batches and four threads, the CNN pipeline was faster than RF at both the median (57.8 versus 104.2~ms) and the 95th percentile (109.6 versus 132.8~ms). Future work will connect a physical 12-lead recorder, compare against tuned gradient-boosting baselines under nested selection, and validate on an independent multicenter cohort.
